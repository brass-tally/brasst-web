# Brasstally

**Books for people who run a company and a life.**

Bookkeeping for owner-operated Canadian companies. Your business and your
personal finances are kept as two separate records rather than one pile with a
filter on it, because they are two separate things and only one of them files a
T2.

As at 11 September 2026. GENIE AI, Inc., Ontario, Canada.

---

## Getting money into the books

**Photograph a receipt.** Vendor, amount, date, tax and a suggested category
are read off the page and the form arrives filled in. Every field stays
editable, because a model reading a crumpled receipt can be wrong and you are
the one signing the return.

**Or type it in plain words.** "paid Vercel $70 today" becomes an entry with a
category and a date.

**A receipt can settle the bill it pays.** If a photographed receipt matches an
open payable by party and amount, Brasstally offers to settle that bill rather
than adding a second entry, and the receipt becomes the evidence.

**Import a statement.** Paste or upload, then review line by line with
duplicates caught before they land.

**It learns how you file.** Categorise an unmatched bank line once and the same
line is filed the same way next month. Recognition ignores reference numbers
and dates, so a monthly fee is matched across instances.

## Seeing where you stand

**Snapshot.** Balance, net for the month, money in and out, owed to you, you
owe. Choose which cards you want. One bar underneath shows the month's shape
with a sentence saying what it means.

**What wants you.** Three panels above the numbers: what needs a decision, how
close the month is to closed, and the reports you keep returning to. The
closing checklist is worked out from your books rather than ticked by hand, so
the percentage is a fact rather than a record of your diligence.

**Transactions.** Search across description, category and subcategory, filter
by direction and account, edit in place, attach documents, transfer between
ledgers, set entries to recur.

**Receivables and payables.** Who owes you and what you owe, grouped by party
with running totals. Settling asks for a receipt, and a settled entry locks.

**Credits.** Non-cash pools such as cloud credits, tracked beside cash. What is
left leads, what it bought sits underneath, and none of it touches your bank
balance.

**Calendar.** A month at a glance, plus what is expected in and out over the
next thirty days.

**Profit and loss.** Revenue, expenses and net with month-over-month change,
category breakdown, six-month trend and average daily burn.

**Reports.** Any period cut into a statement, the months behind it, and where
the money went. Every block exports.

## The bank

**Connect through Plaid.** You sign in on your bank's own screen. Brasstally
never sees your banking credentials, and the access it receives can read
transactions and balances and cannot move money.

**Consolidate.** Pairing what the bank saw with what your books say, so every
difference has a name. Brasstally states its plan before it runs, does the
certain work itself and asks about the rest.

**Duplicates, handled in two tiers.** Identical entries on the same day with
the same reference are removed automatically and recorded. Anything ambiguous
is asked about. A cluster too large to be copies of one payment is reported
rather than deleted.

**Every run is recorded**, so a consolidation can be explained months later.

**A morning pass.** After the overnight sync, the pairs Brasstally is certain
about are made for you and reported in one message. Nothing is created,
deleted or settled without you.

## Tax

**GST and HST per receipt**, read as printed rather than calculated, with the
regime recorded so the recoverable share is known. It knows the provincial
portion is not recoverable in British Columbia or Saskatchewan.

**The rules applied as you go.** Meals halved on both the deduction and the
credit, because CRA limits them together. Capital separated from expense
against a threshold you adopt, with a CCA class suggested.

**A tax pack.** A period's figures, every category on the CRA line it belongs
to, capital additions with their classes, and the net tax position owing or
refundable. One file for your accountant, ending with your capitalisation
policy, registration status and province.

**Receipts on file**, plus the count of entries without one and the total at
risk.

**Corporate and personal drafts.** Your year mapped onto GIFI lines with
deadlines tracked, and a T1 working paper for what CRA cannot see.

## Tally, your bookkeeper

**Ask about your money.** Twenty-two tools over your own ledger: variance,
trends, obligations, balance breakdown, duplicates, recurring costs, cash
forecast, data quality.

**She proposes, you decide.** A transaction, an obligation, a settlement, a
budget, a contact, an intake link or an invitation to a supplier. Each arrives
as a card and nothing happens until you tap it.

**She speaks once.** At most one message, only when something is worth saying,
and never the same thing twice.

**Answers use tables**, not paragraphs, so four overdue invoices are a glance
rather than something you read twice.

## Working with other people

**Contacts.** Everyone the ledger deals with, filed as contractor, vendor,
employee, client or accountant. Every party and email field offers them,
narrowed to the roles that make sense there.

**Read access for your accountant.** Per ledger, by email address. They are
emailed an invitation, open it, receive a six digit code and are in. No account
to create and no password to choose. They can read and export everything and
change nothing, and they never see your bank connection.

**An intake link for suppliers.** Send a contractor a link and their invoice
arrives in a tray with the PDF attached. Both sides are emailed. Accepting
creates an ordinary payable; nothing reaches your books unaccepted.

**Monthly arrangements.** A contractor marks a submission as monthly and it is
raised for them from then on, so they stop re-typing the same invoice. It still
lands in your tray for you to accept.

**Send one back.** "Needs correction" emails them the reason and a link, and
the invoice stays pending until the corrected one arrives.

## The app itself

Runs in a browser and installs to a phone. Four palettes in light and dark,
every combination contrast-checked. Everything exports to CSV without asking
us. A privacy policy, terms of use and a plain-language page on how financial
data is handled, all readable inside the app.

## What Brasstally does not do

Stated here rather than discovered later.

**It is not an accountant.** It prepares figures and shows its working. Nothing
in it is accounting, tax or legal advice.

**It cannot file your return.** No Canadian tax software exposes a filing
interface to third parties. Anyone promising one-click filing is transmitting
through certified software you could have used directly. Brasstally prepares
the package and tracks the return through draft, sent, filed and assessed.

**It never moves money.** Bank connections are read only.

**It does not sell your data or show advertising**, and your ledger is not used
to train models.

## Not built yet

**Payroll.** A business with employees needs a payroll product alongside this.

**Invoices you send.** Brasstally records what you are owed but does not issue
the invoice.

**Multi-currency.** One currency per ledger.

**Push notifications.** Email and an in-app mark today; they do not reach a
closed phone.
